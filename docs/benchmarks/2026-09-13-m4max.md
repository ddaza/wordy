## Quality observations

No reference transcript exists for this lecture, so word error rate was not measured. Reading matched passages (e.g. 10:00–11:15):

- `small` is noticeably more coherent than `base` on this non-native-English speaker with classroom noise; `base` produces more word salad in cross-talk.
- `small-q5_1` output reads very close to `small` in the sampled passages, at two-thirds of the memory and 40 % of the download size. A proper WER comparison is still needed before quantized `small` is preferred by default.
- All models produce roll-call fragments and hesitations as short passages in the first two minutes; that is faithful to the audio.

### Boundary defect found and fixed

Comparing `base` and `small` at the 11:00 chunk boundary showed the first reconciler (midpoint attribution) dropped a sentence that started at 10:58 and ended at 11:02: chunk 10 discarded it because its midpoint lay past 11:00, and chunk 11, which began mid-sentence at 10:57, did not re-emit it. `ChunkReconciler` now commits every segment that *starts* inside a chunk's owned range (that chunk heard the sentence with 3 s of trailing context) and trims the next chunk's re-hearing by exact boundary-word match or, if the two transcripts disagree, time-proportionally. The 60s+3s rows above were re-run after the fix; passage counts rose by 2–4 % (recovered boundary speech) with unchanged timing. Tests: `Tests/ChunkPipelineTests.swift` ("straddling", "disagreeing boundary", "deliberate repetition").

## Policy comparison

- **30 s** chunks: fastest first result, most whisper windows, slowest overall (each 30 s chunk is still padded to whisper's 30 s window plus overlap, so relative overhead is highest).
- **60 s** chunks: best balance; first passage under 1 s on this machine; one checkpoint per minute of audio so a crash loses ≤ 60 s of work.
- **300 s** chunks: lowest overhead for `base`, but first result takes 2–4 s here and would take tens of seconds on an M1 or Intel CPU, and a crash recomputes up to 5 minutes. Not appropriate for the interactive default.

## Provisional recommendation (Apple Silicon; pending M1 and Intel runs)

- Default policy **60 s + 3 s overlap**.
- Default model **`small`** on Apple Silicon: full lecture in 80 s here; scaling by the published M1/M4 Max ratio for whisper.cpp Metal (roughly 4–6×), a two-hour lecture should land around 6–10 minutes on an 8 GB M1, well inside the 30-minute budget — but this is an extrapolation until measured.
- Keep **`base`** as the Intel default candidate. `small` CPU-only at RTF 0.04 on M4 would already be ~0.3–0.5 on a 2017 quad-core Intel by rough scaling, i.e. 40–60 minutes for two hours; `base` is the only candidate likely to meet the Intel budget, and even that must be measured.
- Evaluate `small-q5_1` as the Apple Silicon default once a WER comparison exists; memory (≈ 800 MB worker footprint vs ≈ 1.15 GB) matters on 8 GB machines.

## In-app run through the XPC worker (2026-09-14, Release, `base`, 60s+3s)

Launched with `--open … --install-model whisper-base`: model download + SHA-256 verification took 9 s; the job then ran 69 chunks at ≈ 0.35 s each before the worker was killed. After quit and relaunch it resumed at chunk 68 and completed the remaining 32 chunks (2095 s of audio) in 15.3 s: in-app RTF **0.0073**, first restored-run result 1.55 s, peak worker footprint **556 MB**, main-thread scheduling delay **max 16.4 ms, p95 1.06 ms** sampled at 10 Hz (this session had no concurrent playback). The XPC hop adds no measurable overhead over the in-process harness (RTF 0.0073 both ways).
